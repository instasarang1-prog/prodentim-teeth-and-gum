Tooth and gum pain can have many possible causes. Learn what may be behind the discomfort, what you can do while arranging care and which symptoms should not be ignored.

Why Do My Teeth and Gums Hurt?

If you are wondering why your teeth and gums hurt, there is no single answer. Discomfort can come from the teeth themselves, the gums or surrounding tissues.

Common possibilities include tooth decay, gum inflammation, sensitivity, food trapped between teeth, irritation from brushing, an injury or other dental conditions.

The location, duration and severity of the pain can provide clues, but they cannot reliably determine the cause on their own.

Important: Persistent tooth or gum pain should not be treated as something to simply manage with home remedies or supplements. A dental professional can identify the underlying cause.
Possible Cause 1

Tooth Decay

Tooth decay can eventually cause sensitivity or pain, particularly when the affected tooth is exposed to hot, cold or sweet foods and drinks.

If pain is recurring or becoming more noticeable, a dental examination can determine whether decay or another problem is involved.

Possible Cause 2

Gum Inflammation

Irritated or inflamed gums may feel tender, swollen or uncomfortable. Bleeding during brushing or cleaning between the teeth can also be a sign that your gums need attention.

Improving daily plaque control is important, but persistent symptoms should be assessed by a dental professional.

Possible Cause 3

Tooth Sensitivity

Some people experience short, sharp discomfort when consuming hot, cold, sweet or acidic foods and drinks.

Sensitivity can have several possible causes, so ongoing or severe sensitivity should not simply be assumed to be normal.

Possible Cause 4

Food Trapped Between Teeth

Food particles can become trapped between teeth and irritate the surrounding gums. This can sometimes create localized tenderness or discomfort.

Gentle cleaning between the teeth may help remove trapped debris. Avoid forcing sharp objects into the gums.

Possible Cause 5

Injury or Irritation

A recent injury, biting something unusually hard or irritation from dental appliances can sometimes cause localized discomfort.

If pain continues after the initial irritation should have settled, professional assessment is appropriate.

Possible Cause 6

Teeth Grinding or Clenching

Repeatedly clenching or grinding the teeth can place additional pressure on the teeth and jaw.

If you regularly wake with jaw discomfort, tooth tenderness or headaches, mention these symptoms to your dentist.

Possible Cause 7

Other Dental Problems

Pain can also be associated with problems such as a damaged filling, a cracked tooth, an infection or other conditions affecting the mouth.

These conditions cannot be reliably diagnosed from symptoms alone. A dental examination may be needed to determine what is happening.

What Should You Do When Your Teeth and Gums Hurt?

If the discomfort is mild and you are arranging a dental appointment, focus on gentle everyday oral care rather than aggressive cleaning.

  1. Brush gently with fluoride toothpaste.
  2. Clean between your teeth carefully.
  3. Avoid repeatedly exposing the painful area to very hot, cold or sugary foods if these trigger discomfort.
  4. Avoid tobacco products.
  5. Arrange professional dental care if the problem persists, worsens or keeps returning.

When Should You See a Dentist?

Persistent tooth or gum pain deserves professional attention, particularly when the symptoms are worsening or accompanied by other changes.

Contact a dental professional if you have:
  • Persistent or worsening tooth pain
  • Swollen or painful gums
  • Repeated gum bleeding
  • A loose tooth
  • Pus around a tooth or gum
  • Significant sensitivity
  • A damaged or cracked tooth
  • Pain that keeps returning
Seek urgent medical or dental care for serious symptoms.

Significant facial swelling, difficulty breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding or severe rapidly worsening symptoms require prompt professional attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do my teeth and gums hurt?

Tooth and gum pain can have several causes, including tooth decay, gum inflammation, sensitivity, trauma, food trapped between teeth or other dental problems. Persistent or severe pain should be evaluated by a dental professional.

Why do my teeth and gums hurt when I brush?

Pain while brushing can be associated with irritated gums, tooth sensitivity, brushing too aggressively or an underlying dental problem. A dentist can help determine the cause if the problem persists.

Can gum problems cause tooth pain?

Gum problems can be associated with discomfort around the teeth, although tooth pain can also have other causes. Persistent pain should be assessed by a dental professional.

Should I use a supplement for tooth or gum pain?

A supplement should not be used as a substitute for identifying the cause of persistent tooth or gum pain. Dental problems may require professional diagnosis and treatment.

When should I see a dentist for tooth and gum pain?

Seek dental care for persistent or worsening pain, swelling, bleeding, loose teeth, pus, fever or other concerning symptoms.
