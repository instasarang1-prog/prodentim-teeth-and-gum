Healthy teeth and gums are built through consistent daily habits. Learn the simple routines that can support oral health and the warning signs that should be checked by a dental professional.

What Does Healthy Oral Care Look Like?

Healthy teeth and gums are supported by more than one habit. Brushing, cleaning between the teeth, diet, fluoride exposure and professional dental care all have a role in maintaining oral health.

The most useful approach is consistency. A complicated routine that you rarely follow is less practical than a simple routine you can maintain every day.

Think long-term: Oral health is usually about maintaining good habits consistently, rather than looking for a single product that does everything.
Habit 1

Brush Twice a Day

Brushing helps remove plaque and food particles from the surfaces of your teeth.

A common routine is brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste. Use a gentle technique rather than applying excessive pressure.

Habit 2

Clean Between Your Teeth

Toothbrush bristles cannot effectively reach every space between teeth. Cleaning these areas can help remove plaque and trapped debris.

Depending on your individual needs, options may include dental floss or interdental brushes.

Habit 3

Use Fluoride Toothpaste

Fluoride helps protect teeth against decay. Using fluoride toothpaste as part of your regular brushing routine can therefore support tooth health.

Follow the instructions on your toothpaste and ask your dentist if you have specific questions about fluoride use.

Habit 4

Watch Your Sugar Frequency

Frequently exposing your teeth to sugary foods and drinks can contribute to an environment where tooth decay can develop.

Limiting frequent sugary snacks and drinks can make maintaining healthy teeth easier.

Habit 5

Drink Water Regularly

Water is a simple everyday beverage choice and can be especially useful when replacing sugary drinks.

Staying hydrated also supports general health, while drinking water instead of frequently consuming sugary beverages can reduce repeated sugar exposure to your teeth.

Habit 6

Avoid Tobacco

Tobacco use can negatively affect oral health and is associated with gum disease and other oral problems.

Avoiding tobacco is an important part of maintaining healthier gums and supporting overall health.

Habit 7

Protect Your Teeth From Unnecessary Damage

Teeth are strong, but they can still be damaged by accidents, excessive pressure or habits such as chewing very hard objects.

If you participate in activities where dental injuries are possible, ask a dental professional about appropriate protective equipment.

Habit 8

Keep Regular Dental Appointments

Professional dental visits allow your teeth and gums to be examined for problems that may not be obvious during your normal routine.

Your dentist can also recommend cleaning intervals and other care based on your individual oral-health needs.

Habit 9

Pay Attention to Changes

Do not ignore changes such as persistent bleeding, swelling, sensitivity, pain, gum recession or loose teeth.

Recognizing changes early gives you an opportunity to discuss them with a dental professional.

Common Oral-Care Mistakes

  • Brushing too aggressively.
  • Cleaning only the visible surfaces of your teeth.
  • Frequently consuming sugary snacks and drinks.
  • Ignoring persistent gum bleeding or swelling.
  • Using tobacco products.
  • Skipping professional dental care.
  • Expecting one product or supplement to replace a complete oral-care routine.
Remember:

Persistent pain, swelling, bleeding, loose teeth or other ongoing symptoms should be evaluated by a dental professional rather than managed solely with home remedies.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I keep my teeth and gums healthy?

Brush regularly with fluoride toothpaste, clean between your teeth, limit frequent sugar exposure, avoid tobacco and maintain regular dental care.

How often should I brush my teeth?

Brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste is a common recommendation for maintaining oral hygiene.

Is flossing important for healthy gums?

Cleaning between the teeth helps remove plaque and debris from areas that a toothbrush may not reach effectively.

Can oral probiotics replace brushing?

No. Oral probiotics should not be considered a replacement for regular brushing, cleaning between the teeth or professional dental care.

When should I see a dentist?

Persistent pain, swelling, bleeding, loose teeth, pus or other ongoing changes should be evaluated by a dental professional.
