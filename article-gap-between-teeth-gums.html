Gap Between Teeth and Gums: Causes, Symptoms & When to See a Dentist

A visible gap around the gumline can have several possible explanations. Learn what may cause it, what symptoms to watch for and when a dental examination is appropriate.

What Does a Gap Between Teeth and Gums Mean?

If you notice a gap between teeth and gums, the first question is whether it is a normal feature of your mouth or a recent change.

The appearance of the gumline can change for several reasons. Gum recession, inflammation, tooth position and normal anatomy can all affect how much space appears around a tooth.

A new or enlarging gap deserves more attention than a stable feature that has always been present.

Key point: You cannot reliably determine the cause of a gumline gap from its appearance alone. A dentist can examine the gums and teeth and determine what is happening.
Possible Cause 1

Gum Recession

Gum recession occurs when the gum tissue moves away from the tooth, exposing more of the tooth surface.

This can change the appearance of the gumline and may sometimes be accompanied by tooth sensitivity.

If you notice that your gums appear to be moving lower or the gap is becoming more noticeable, professional evaluation is appropriate.

Possible Cause 2

Natural Anatomy

Not every visible space around a tooth indicates a disease or dental problem. The shape and position of teeth and gums naturally vary between people.

If the appearance has remained unchanged for a long time and there are no symptoms, it may simply be part of your normal anatomy.

Possible Cause 3

Gum Inflammation and Plaque Buildup

Plaque buildup can irritate the gums and contribute to gum inflammation.

Maintaining regular brushing and cleaning between the teeth is an important part of everyday oral hygiene.

Persistent bleeding, swelling or changes around the gumline should be discussed with a dental professional.

Possible Cause 4

Missing or Shifted Teeth

Changes in tooth position can alter the spaces between teeth and the surrounding gum tissue.

If you notice a new space developing or an existing space changing, a dentist can determine whether tooth movement or another factor is involved.

Possible Cause 5

Injury or Irritation

An injury or repeated irritation can affect the tissues around a tooth.

If a gap appears after an injury or is accompanied by pain, bleeding or tooth movement, arrange a dental assessment.

Symptoms to Watch For

A gap by itself does not explain what caused it. Pay attention to other changes occurring at the same time.

  • Bleeding gums
  • Swollen or tender gums
  • Persistent tooth sensitivity
  • Persistent tooth or gum pain
  • Gum recession
  • Loose teeth
  • Changes in tooth position
  • Pus around the gums or teeth
  • A gap that appears to be getting larger

What Can You Do at Home?

Everyday oral-care habits can support healthier teeth and gums, but they should not be used to self-diagnose the cause of a new gap.

  1. Brush gently with fluoride toothpaste.
  2. Clean between your teeth regularly.
  3. Avoid brushing so aggressively that you irritate your gums.
  4. Limit frequent exposure to sugary foods and drinks.
  5. Avoid tobacco products.
  6. Maintain regular professional dental care.

When Should You See a Dentist?

A dental examination is particularly useful when a gap is new, changing or associated with other symptoms.

Arrange professional dental care if you notice:
  • A new or enlarging gap
  • Bleeding or swollen gums
  • Persistent pain
  • Tooth sensitivity that continues
  • Gum recession
  • A loose tooth
  • Pus around a tooth or gum
  • Changes in tooth position

Severe swelling, difficulty breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding or rapidly worsening symptoms require prompt professional attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes a gap between teeth and gums?

The appearance of a gap can have different causes, including normal tooth anatomy, gum recession, changes in the gums or spaces created by missing or shifted teeth. A dentist can determine the cause.

Can gum recession create a gap between teeth and gums?

Gum recession can expose more of the tooth and change the appearance of the space around the gumline. A dental professional can assess the cause and severity.

Can you fix a gap between teeth and gums naturally?

Home oral-care habits can support gum health, but they cannot reliably correct structural changes or determine the cause of a new gap. A dentist should evaluate persistent or changing gaps.

Can oral probiotics close a gap between teeth and gums?

Oral probiotics should not be presented as a way to physically correct a structural gap or reverse gum recession. The cause of the gap should be evaluated first.

When should I see a dentist about a gap?

See a dentist if a gap is new, becoming larger, associated with bleeding or pain, or accompanied by gum recession, loose teeth or other changes.
